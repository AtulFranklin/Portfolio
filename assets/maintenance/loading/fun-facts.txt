# The first website went live at CERN in 1991.

The first logged "computer bug" was a real moth, taped into the Harvard Mark II logbook in 1947.
Ada Lovelace published what's often called the first computer program in 1843, for Babbage's Analytical Engine.
Python is named after Monty Python's Flying Circus, not the snake.
Brendan Eich wrote the first version of JavaScript in about 10 days in 1995.
The first website went live at CERN in 1991, and you can still visit it at info.cern.ch.
The Apollo 11 guidance computer had about 4 KB of RAM.
Margaret Hamilton led the team that wrote the Apollo on-board flight software.
Grace Hopper's A-0 system from 1952 is often called the first compiler.
FORTRAN, released in 1957, is still used for scientific computing today.
Linus Torvalds announced Linux in 1991 as "just a hobby, won't be big and professional".
Linus Torvalds also wrote Git, in 2005, for the Linux kernel's own source code.
Unix time counts seconds since January 1, 1970, and 32-bit counters run out on January 19, 2038.
The Y2K bug came from storing years as two digits to save memory.
"Hello, world!" became famous through Brian Kernighan's C tutorials in the 1970s.
The first .com domain ever registered was symbolics.com, on March 15, 1985.
Ray Tomlinson picked the @ sign for email addresses in 1971.
The first computer mouse, built for Douglas Engelbart in the 1960s, had a wooden shell.
Dart, the language behind Flutter, was first shown by Google in 2011.
Stack Overflow launched in 2008.
US stocks traded in fractions like 1/16 of a dollar until decimal prices took over in 2001.
A nibble is half a byte: 4 bits.
"There are only two hard things in Computer Science: cache invalidation and naming things." - Phil Karlton
