lead: I'm a Computer Science master's student at Stevens who trades the markets and builds the software behind them. Most of what I make started as a problem I had myself.
paragraph: My undergrad degree is in mechanical engineering, and that's where I learned to think in a systemic manner. I modelled parts in CAD, ran crash simulations to find where a car body fails and wrote physics simulators in Python. The part I kept enjoying most was the code logic and problem solving, so I made the switch and brought the habits with me: understand the whole system, expect things to break and test before you trust it.
paragraph: At Whirlpool I saw what a small piece of software can do inside a big company. Bill-of-materials pricing was being kept up by hand every month, so I automated it with Google Apps Script and cut that work by more than half. The internship also taught me the unglamorous side of shipping: validation testing, corrective actions and getting marketing, process and quality teams to agree on a change.
paragraph: I trade stocks, intraday and futures & options myself, so I know what a trading tool has to get right. Trade on Paper began as a way to practise Indian markets without risking money and grew into a full simulator: 5x intraday leverage, margin tracking, auto square-off, a Black-Scholes option chain and an automated scalping engine with daily stop limits. If one price feed goes down it falls back to the next, because in markets "mostly works" isn't good enough.
paragraph: I learn new tools by building something real with them. This year that meant Flutter, Kotlin, Firebase, a browser extension with local encryption and language models running fully offline, on a phone and on desktop using Ollama. I use AI-assisted development to move faster and enhance my workflow when needed, but I still own the design, read what goes in and test what comes out. Having run these models myself, I know where they're sharp and where they need a second look.
paragraph: If you're building trading systems, fintech or software that can't afford to break, I'd like to hear about it.
